Heat treatment is a standard industry practice used to optimize the color and clarity of natural sapphires. Upward of 90 percent of commercial sapphires undergo thermal enhancement before entering retail markets. Treatment techniques range from low-temperature heating to high-temperature atmosphere-controlled furnace processing.

Low-temperature heating (between 600 and 1,000 degrees Celsius) is often applied to pink and purple sapphires to lighten dark undertones or remove brownish secondary hues. High-temperature heating (between 1,400 and 1,850 degrees Celsius) causes structural alterations in blue sapphires. At these elevated temperatures, internal rutile silk dissolves into the crystal matrix, releasing titanium ions that react with iron ions to create intense blue color centers via intervalence charge transfer.

Additionally, high-temperature heating under reducing atmospheres can transform pale, cloudy "Geuda" corundum from Sri Lanka into vibrant, transparent blue gems. Advanced treatments also include beryllium lattice diffusion, where lightness or orange-yellow tones are artificially induced by heating corundum with beryllium oxides at near-melting temperatures.

Advanced gemological laboratories utilize advanced spectroscopy tools—such as UV-Vis-NIR absorption and micro-Raman spectroscopy—to detect subtle lattice distortions, dissolved silk residues, and element diffusion profiles. Clear disclosure of treatment category remains mandatory across international gemstone trade standard organizations.
