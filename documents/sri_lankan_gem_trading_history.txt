Sri Lanka boasts over two millennia of documented gem mining and maritime trade history. Known anciently by Arab merchants as "Serendib," by Greco-Roman traders as "Taprobane," and by ancient chroniclers as "Rathna Dweepa" (Island of Gems), the island occupied a pivotal position on ancient Maritime Silk Road trading routes connecting East Asia, the Middle East, and the Mediterranean.

Historical texts document the early global prestige of Ceylon gems. Second-century astronomer Ptolemy recorded the abundance of sapphires and beryls on the island. Arab traveler Ibn Battuta, visiting in the 14th century, detailed royal courts adorned with large rubies and cat's eye gems extracted from the slopes of Adam's Peak. Venetian explorer Marco Polo famously declared Sri Lanka to have the finest rubies, sapphires, topaz, and amethysts in the world.

Sri Lanka's gemstone trade fostered rich multicultural exchanges. Arab traders settled along coastal commercial ports such as Beruwala and Galle, establishing merchant dynasties that remain central to the country's gem trading ecosystem today. Gemstones were exchanged for silk, spices, ceramics, and precious metals.

This historical legacy endures in modern Sri Lanka, where traditional gem trading centers—such as the open-air street markets of China Fort in Beruwala and Ratnapura—continue centuries-old bargaining customs alongside contemporary digital international auctions.
