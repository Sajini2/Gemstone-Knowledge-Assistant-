Thermal enhancement is the most prevalent commercial treatment applied to natural rubies to improve color intensity and clarity. Heating rubies at high temperatures alters trace element oxidation states and dissolves internal rutile inclusions, resulting in a cleaner and more saturated red appearance.

Conventional thermal treatment involves placing rough rubies in controlled electric or gas furnaces at temperatures ranging from 1,200 to 1,800 degrees Celsius. At temperatures exceeding 1,400 degrees Celsius, fine rutile silk diffuses into the corundum lattice, releasing titanium ions that interact with iron to reduce unwanted brown or blue secondary tones. This process also heals microscopic surface-reaching fractures by dissolving ambient silica or borax fluxes added during heating, a procedure known as flux-assisted heat treatment or fracture healing.

More aggressive enhancements include lead-glass filling, which treats highly fractured low-grade rubies. In this method, the material is etched with hydrofluoric acid to clear fracture debris and subsequently baked with high-lead silicate glass powder. The glass molten phase fills surface fractures and cavities, matching the refractive index of corundum (1.76 to 1.77) to conceal optical flaws.

Independent gemological testing laboratories distinguish untreated rubies from heat-treated and glass-filled stones using Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FTIR), ultraviolet fluorescence imaging, and microscopic inspection of internal stress features. Treated rubies must be transparently disclosed to consumers because enhancements significantly impact commercial market value.
