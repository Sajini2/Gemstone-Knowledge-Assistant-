Sri Lanka, historically nicknamed "Rathna Dweepa" (Island of Gems), yields an astonishing diversity of gemstone species within a concentrated geographic footprint. Beyond classic rubies and blue sapphires, the island is famed for unique mineral species and unusual gem varieties.

A notable local gem variety is "Geuda" corundum—translucent to milky-white sapphires containing high concentrations of titanium silk. Through specialized heat treatment, Sri Lankan gem artisans transform once low-value Geuda rough into vivid blue sapphires, revolutionizing the global sapphire market.

Sri Lanka is also the type locality for rare mineral species such as Sinhalite and Ekanite. Sinhalite (a magnesium aluminum borate) was historically misidentified as brown chrysoberyl until detailed crystallographic analysis confirmed it as a distinct mineral species in 1952. Ekanite, discovered in 1953 in Eheliyagoda, is a rare radioactive silicate mineral containing thorium and uranium that exhibits striking metamict crystalline breakdown.

Additionally, Sri Lanka produces exceptionally fine color-changing Chrysoberyl Alexandrite, vibrant cobalt blue Spinel, yellowish-green Chrysoberyl Cat's Eye (displaying sharp chatoyancy), Zircon, Topaz, Tourmaline, and Taaffeite. This immense mineralogical diversity stems from the island's complex precambrian metamorphic basement geology and long thermal history.
