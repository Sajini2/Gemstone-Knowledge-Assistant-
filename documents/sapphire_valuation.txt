The commercial valuation of sapphires hinges on color saturation, transparency, weight, cut quality, and geographic origin. Color is the single most critical factor, evaluated through hue, tone, and saturation. Premium blue sapphires showcase a rich cornflower blue to vivid royal blue hue with medium-dark tone and vivid saturation, unmarred by greyish or greenish overtones.

Geographic origin exerts a profound influence on pricing. Historic Kashmir sapphires, renowned for their velvety cornflower blue appearance caused by microscopic liquid inclusions, command the highest price per carat in global markets. Burmese sapphires, prized for intense royal blue hues, also command substantial premiums. Ceylon (Sri Lankan) sapphires are highly esteemed for their brilliant light-to-medium cornflower blue color and exceptional optical clarity.

Clarity standards for sapphires are generally higher than for rubies. Inclusion-free, eye-clean sapphires with vibrant color saturation are exceptionally rare in sizes exceeding five carats. Price increases exponentially with carat weight, particularly for untreated natural gems.

Furthermore, cut precision dramatically impacts market value. Proper pavilion depth prevents "windows"—pale, unreflective central areas—and ensures maximum brilliance. Independent gemological certification verifying natural origin and certifying the absence of heat treatment is essential for securing high-tier investment values.
