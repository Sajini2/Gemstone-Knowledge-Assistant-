Sri Lanka is widely recognized as a global leader in ethical, sustainable gem mining and responsible sourcing. Unlike large-scale industrial open-pit operations in other regions, Sri Lanka's gem sector is regulated to prioritize environmental protection, community welfare, and sustainable resource extraction.

The National Gem and Jewellery Authority (NGJA) enforces strict licensing protocols for all mining operations. Environmental regulations prohibit heavy open-pit machinery and chemical processing in alluvial river basins. Mining licenses mandate traditional pit extraction methods, restricting site footprints and preventing widespread deforestation and soil erosion.

A hallmark of Sri Lanka's sustainable model is mandatory post-mining land rehabilitation. Before a mining permit is granted, operators must deposit an environmental security bond. Upon completion of gravel extraction, miners are legally required to backfill vertical shafts and horizontal shafts using saved topsoil, restoring the terrain for agricultural cultivation such as paddy farming or rubber planting.

Socially, Sri Lankan mining laws safeguard miner welfare. Traditional pit operations function under profit-sharing cooperative structures ("Karuhawula"), where miners receive fair revenue shares from gem sales alongside daily wages. Furthermore, child labor is strictly banned, and workplace safety compliance is routinely monitored. This comprehensive legal framework ensures that Sri Lankan gemstones are ethically mined from earth to market.
