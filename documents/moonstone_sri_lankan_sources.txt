Sri Lanka is world-renowned as the primary historical and commercial source of top-grade blue moonstone. The principal mining locality is concentrated around the village of Meetiyagoda, situated in the Southern Province near Ambalangoda. The Meetiyagoda deposit represents a unique pegmatitic weathered kaolin pipe formation that yields exceptionally transparent orthoclase-albite feldspar crystals.

Geologically, the moonstones of Meetiyagoda originated within highly differentiated granitic pegmatites intruding precambrian metamorphic host rocks. Over millions of years, deep tropical weathering decomposed the host feldspars and surrounding matrix into soft kaolin clay beds, preserving intact gem-quality moonstone nodules within residual alluvial gravels (locally known as "illam").

Mining at Meetiyagoda is conducted primarily through traditional shaft mining techniques. Miners excavate vertical wooden-shored shafts down to depths of 10 to 30 meters to reach the water-bearing kaolin gravel layers. The kaolin-rich gravel is hauled to the surface using manual winches, washed in nearby pits using woven bamboo baskets, and hand-sorted to extract moonstone rough.

The Meetiyagoda mines produce moonstones celebrated for their unmatched transparency and vivid, concentrated blue adularescence. Because local reserves are finite and deep shafts present hydrological challenges, fine Meetiyagoda blue moonstones command significant valuation premiums in global gem auctions.
