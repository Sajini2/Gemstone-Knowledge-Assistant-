Moonstone is a popular gem in fine jewelry, admired for its romantic luster and unique optical properties. It was extensively featured in late 19th-century Art Nouveau jewelry by masters such as René Lalique and Louis Comfort Tiffany, as well as in mid-20th-century retro and contemporary artisan designs.

From a practical jewelry perspective, moonstone requires thoughtful setting design and delicate handling. Moonstone registers between 6.0 and 6.5 on the Mohs scale of mineral hardness, making it softer than quartz, ruby, and sapphire. Furthermore, feldspar possesses two directional cleavage planes meeting at approximately 90-degree angles. Consequently, moonstone is susceptible to chipping, scratching, or cleaving upon sharp impact.

Jewelry designers frequently set moonstones into protective bezel or halo settings rather than exposed prong configurations. It is ideally suited for earrings, brooches, pendants, and protective pins where surface abrasion risk is low. When set into daily-wear rings, protective metal borders around the cabochon perimeter help shield the stone from impact.

Care instructions emphasize avoiding ultrasonic cleaners and steam processing, which can induce thermal shock or split cleavage planes. Moonstone jewelry should be cleaned gently using warm soapy water and a soft microfiber cloth, and stored separately from harder gemstones like diamonds and corundum.
