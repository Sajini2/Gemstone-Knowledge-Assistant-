Padparadscha sapphire is an exceptionally rare and highly prized fancy corundum variety defined by a delicate combination of pink and orange hues. The name derives from the Sinhalese word "padma raga," meaning lotus blossom, reflecting the stone's color resemblance to a tropical aquatic bloom or a fiery sunset.

Gemologically, an authentic Padparadscha sapphire must display a light to medium tone with a subtle, harmonious blend of pink and orange. If either color dominates excessively, the gem is classified as a pink or orange sapphire rather than a Padparadscha. The dual coloration arises from a precise geochemical balance of trace chromium (inducing pink) and iron-related color centers or trace beryllium (inducing orange).

Historically sourced exclusively from Sri Lanka's alluvial gem gravels, fine natural Padparadscha sapphires have also been recovered in Vietnam, Madagascar, and Tanzania. Due to their extreme natural scarcity, prices per carat rival high-grade blue sapphires and rubies.

Consumers and buyers must exercise caution regarding artificial color enhancement. High-temperature beryllium lattice diffusion treatment can induce a superficial pinkish-orange color in pale corundum. Laboratory analysis using Laser Ablation Inductively Coupled Plasma Mass Spectrometry (LA-ICP-MS) is necessary to detect trace beryllium concentrations and differentiate natural Padparadscha sapphires from artificially treated stones.
