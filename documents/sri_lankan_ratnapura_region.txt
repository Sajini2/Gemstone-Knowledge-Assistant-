Ratnapura, known in Sinhalese as the "City of Gems," is the historical heartland of Sri Lanka's world-renowned gemstone industry. Situated in the southwestern Sabaragamuwa Province, the Ratnapura basin lies within a fertile valley fed by the Kalu River system, acting as a catchment zone for gem-rich sediment washed down from surrounding mountain ranges.

The region's gem wealth is hosted primarily within alluvial gravel beds locally termed "illam." Over millions of years, tropical weathering eroded precambrian metamorphic crystalline rocks containing corundum, chrysoberyl, spinel, tourmaline, and garnet. Rivers transported these heavy mineral grains down valley slopes, concentrating them within ancient river beds beneath layers of clay, sand, and organic mud.

Mining in Ratnapura relies predominantly on traditional, low-impact artisan techniques. "Goda illam" (dry land mining) involves sinking timber-braced vertical shafts to reach gravel seams located 5 to 20 meters below the surface. Horizontal tunnels ("donas") radiate from the shaft base to extract gravel. In riverbeds, "Dobe illam" (river mining) uses long wooden poles and woven baskets to dredge alluvial sediment.

Washing pit operations employ circular woven bamboo baskets ("yatti") rotated manually in river currents. Water washes away lighter silt while heavy gemstone minerals concentrate at the basket center. This eco-conscious placer mining framework minimizes heavy mechanized land destruction while sustaining local artisan livelihoods.
